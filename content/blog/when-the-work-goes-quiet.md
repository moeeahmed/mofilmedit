Every freelancer has quiet weeks, and I'm no different. I've stopped treating them as a bad sign and started treating them as the time I build the next few months of work.

## Start with people who already know you

Most of my work has come from word of mouth and people coming back for more. So when it goes quiet, the first thing I do is message past clients. I don't send a sales pitch. I just check in, ask how the business is going and send them something useful, like an idea for their next event or a quick clip they could use. Often there's a project they've been putting off, and that message is what gets it booked.

## Use the downtime to make my own content

When I'm busy with client work, my own content is the first thing to slip. Quiet weeks are when I catch up. I film short videos in the office about editing, camera settings and how I approach a shoot, and I put them out regularly. People rarely book a videographer they've never seen. Showing up consistently means that when someone needs a video, I'm already the person they've been watching.

## Reach out on purpose, and follow up

I also pick a type of client I really want to work with, like athletes and founders who are building something, and I contact a few each week. Each message is personal: I mention something specific about what they're doing and how video could help. I keep every conversation in one place, so I follow up on enquiries and quotes instead of letting them go cold. A lot of good work comes from the second message, not the first.
