Most people assume a good sports video comes down to the camera. It doesn't. It comes down to what happens before you ever press record.

## Know the story before the whistle

Every match, race or event has a story running underneath it — a comeback, a rivalry, a first appearance, a last one. My job before I film anything is to find out what that story actually is, usually by talking to the people involved rather than reading a fixture list. The footage only means something once you know what you're looking for.

## Framing for emotion, not just action

It's tempting to chase every ball, every sprint, every goal. But the moments that make people feel something are usually the ones either side of the action — a coach's reaction on the line, a parent in the crowd, the quiet seconds after a final whistle. I try to leave room in the frame for those.

## Edit for pace, not length

A three-minute edit that never lets up beats a six-minute one that drifts. I cut hard, keep the rhythm matched to the energy of the event, and never let a shot sit on screen longer than it's earning its place.
